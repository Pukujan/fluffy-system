# Failure Register

| ID | Failure mode | Impact | Detection | Mitigation / containment | Residual |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FR-001 | Agent starts coding before understanding the product | Polished page markets the wrong problem | Compare page thesis to primary-source incidents and PDD | Product-truth pass before creative generation | Product meaning still needs judgment |
| FR-002 | Copy sounds like generic AI marketing | Low trust, weak recognition, interchangeable positioning | Read-aloud, banned-pattern search, specificity review | Dedicated human-marketing rewrite, buyer-language research, examples | Taste remains subjective |
| FR-003 | Copy avoids banned words but keeps AI-shaped structure | Output still feels synthetic | Inspect paragraph rhythm, rhetorical symmetry, repeated section patterns | Structural de-pattern pass, varied sentence/paragraph forms, concrete nouns | Models can develop new stylistic tells over time |
| FR-004 | Claude Code misses the workflow | User runs repo and gets generic output | Test from a clean Claude Code session | Root `CLAUDE.md` imports playbook, research, copy rules, and examples | Claude behavior can change across releases |
| FR-005 | Market gap is inferred only from repo architecture | Story is technically correct and commercially irrelevant | Ask what buyer does today and why they would switch | Live competitor, status-quo, audience-language, and why-now research | Early products may have ambiguous segments |
| FR-006 | External research becomes product proof | Misleading efficacy or traction claim | Claim-source review | Keep external market support separate from product results | Wording still requires care |
| FR-007 | Research is present but does not shape the page | Repo looks rigorous while marketing stays generic | Trace hero/sections back to research insights | Require buyer language, market gap, and at least one consequential research insight in creative brief | Some research may correctly be omitted from final copy |
| FR-008 | Brand research is a link dump | Design remains generic | Compare reference notes to candidate direction | Analyze emotion, composition, typography, product visualization, motion, lessons | Taste cannot be reduced to checklist compliance |
| FR-009 | Candidate set differs mostly by palette | Gallery creates false choice | Thumbnail comparison | Vary story order, layout, typography, metaphor, motion, density, 2D/3D | Product truth creates some shared content |
| FR-010 | Product is hidden behind abstract visuals | Visitor remembers the aesthetic but not what the product does | Five-second comprehension test | Show real workflow, artifact, state change, or product demo early | Some infrastructure products remain abstract by nature |
| FR-011 | Three.js becomes decorative particles | Added weight with no explanatory payoff | Ask what 3D teaches that HTML cannot | Require product-specific spatial/transformation metaphor | 3D novelty can still bias subjective review |
| FR-012 | Three.js import or WebGL fails | Broken hero or unusable page | Simulate script/import failure | Semantic HTML first, progressive enhancement, reduced-motion path | Device variance remains |
| FR-013 | Copy imitates humanity with slang or fake imperfection | Feels manipulative or childish | Voice review | Use specificity, audience context, examples, and natural rhythm | Some brands legitimately use slang |
| FR-014 | Every section ends with a slogan | Page feels written by a model or keynote speaker | Read headlines and paragraph endings in isolation | Remove unnecessary mini-conclusions | Strong brand pages may intentionally use repeated motifs |
| FR-015 | Too many stats turn the story into a report | Reader loses emotional and commercial thread | Count numbers and ask whether each changes perception | Use only high-leverage numbers; move detail to evidence page | Technical buyers may want more data |
| FR-016 | Audience empathy becomes pity | User feels diagnosed or patronized | Target-user critique | Describe the situation and aspiration without making the buyer deficient | Tone can vary by audience |
| FR-017 | Competitors are caricatured to manufacture whitespace | Positioning loses credibility | Compare public competitor claims and current workflow | Acknowledge what alternatives do well; position against real remaining gap | Fast-moving competitors may close gaps |
| FR-018 | AI tool adoption turns into unnecessary platform building | Fluffy becomes another site builder | Reuse assessment and architecture review | Keep Fluffy as repo-guided workflow; use existing renderers/editors | Integration requests may expand scope later |
| FR-019 | OSS tool is credited without executing it | Experiment record is misleading | Adapter/use log | Use `informed by` unless tool actually generated or transformed artifact | Manual inspiration attribution is fuzzy |
| FR-020 | Gallery accidentally becomes featured/public navigation | Experiments look endorsed | Homepage, sitemap, robots/meta review | Unlisted route, noindex, no homepage link | Direct URL remains shareable by design |
| FR-021 | Human owner feels forced to choose a weak winner | Poor work gets promoted | Owner review | `Reject all` is a normal outcome | Requires another iteration |
| FR-022 | Market/brand research becomes stale | Page talks to an old category | Check source and reference dates before major regeneration | Fresh research for each major run | Markets change between updates |
| FR-023 | Evidence/provenance vocabulary becomes the main sales language | Buyer sees an audit report instead of value | First-screen and headline review | Keep provenance as receipts or evidence page unless buyer uses that vocabulary | Assurance products need some technical proof |
| FR-024 | Agent stops after strategy and asks the owner to micromanage | User loses the speed benefit | Compare result to requested deliverables | Default workflow completes candidate generation and gallery publication | Some high-stakes projects need intermediate review |
