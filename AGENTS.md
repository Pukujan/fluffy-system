# Agent operating contract

Read this before doing work for Fluffy System.

## Purpose

Fluffy System is an editorial, research, and creative comparison process. It is not an automated truth engine and it is not allowed to replace human taste with a synthetic score.

The human owner is the final judge of positioning, tone, aesthetics, and candidate selection.

## Required sequence

Do not jump directly from a product repo to HTML.

1. Read the product's own primary sources.
2. Find real incidents, usage, decisions, failures, transcripts, or PRs that reveal why the product exists.
3. Write a plain-language product truth statement with no marketing language.
4. Research the target audience, direct alternatives, adjacent categories, and current market language.
5. Find external research or quantitative evidence that supports the market pain. Keep product proof separate from external support.
6. Form at least three defensible positioning angles and reject weaker ones explicitly.
7. Collect at least 20 useful visual/brand references for a major new direction. Adjacent emotional categories are valid references.
8. Define materially different creative directions.
9. Generate working candidates. Native HTML/CSS/JS is the default control. Include Three.js/WebGL only when it communicates the story, and provide a usable fallback.
10. Publish candidates only to the hidden Design Bakery gallery until the human owner selects one.
11. Run the human-voice, evidence, responsive, accessibility, and interaction passes.
12. Record what was actually used. Never label a page as generated by an OSS tool unless that tool genuinely produced or transformed it.

## Human voice

Prefer a person, a moment, and a recognizable consequence before architecture.

Good public copy tends to follow:

`person -> moment -> tension -> broader pattern -> market gap -> product insight -> proof -> ambition`

Avoid:

- corporate abstraction before the user problem;
- generic AI-startup words such as revolutionary, seamless, empower, unlock, cutting-edge, holistic;
- repeated 'not X, but Y' constructions;
- fake testimonials;
- every section sounding like a polished slogan;
- audit vocabulary on the main marketing page unless the buyer actually speaks that way;
- em dash characters in public marketing copy.

Research should act like a receipt. It should make the story credible without becoming the story.

## Evidence boundaries

Keep these distinct:

- observed product evidence;
- self-reported user evidence;
- derived interpretation;
- external research;
- measured product result;
- unknown or unproven claim.

Never promote one class into another.

## Design freedom

This repo specifies goals and invariants, not page templates.

Each candidate may choose its own:

- narrative emphasis;
- typography;
- composition;
- palette;
- illustration;
- motion;
- 2D/3D rendering;
- section count;
- code organization.

The candidate should feel specific to the product. If its design could move unchanged to an unrelated AI startup, reconsider it.

## Three.js rule

At least one candidate per experiment subject may use Three.js when useful. 3D is an expressive adapter, not a mandatory feature.

A Three.js candidate must:

- retain semantic HTML for core copy;
- keep navigation and CTAs usable without WebGL;
- respect `prefers-reduced-motion`;
- avoid blocking the page if the CDN/module import fails;
- remain usable on touch devices;
- keep text readable on small screens;
- use 3D to explain or embody the product thesis rather than as decorative particles.

## Human review

Never auto-promote a winner to the Design Bakery homepage.

The hidden gallery is the review boundary. The owner may:

- choose a candidate;
- reject all candidates;
- combine ideas;
- ask for another direction;
- dispose of the experiment.

Only after explicit owner selection should a candidate become a featured project.
