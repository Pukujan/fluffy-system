# Fluffy v2 brand reference map

This is the reference set for Fluffy's own brand and for the second-generation gallery.

The point is not to copy these sites. The point is to understand how strong modern products communicate control, creativity, trust, product reality, and visual distinctiveness in an AI-heavy market.

## Direct / adjacent creative tools

### 1. Framer
https://www.framer.com/

What it teaches:
- puts the product canvas and real generated sites close to the hero;
- emphasizes user control and refinement after AI generation;
- uses many visual examples rather than abstract AI iconography;
- treats exploration as a feature of the brand.

Useful for Fluffy:
Show the work. A Fluffy page should reveal the brief, references, copy, and final candidate instead of talking about a pipeline in abstract terms.

### 2. Figma
https://www.figma.com/

What it teaches:
- playful but disciplined visual language;
- product UI is a major part of the marketing surface;
- brand can be colorful without losing professional credibility;
- collaboration and craft are shown through actual interface states.

Useful for Fluffy:
Bring the research and creative process into the visual system. Let the page feel like work happening on a canvas.

### 3. Replit Design
https://replit.com/design

What it teaches:
- design exploration is presented as a live making activity;
- moodboards and references are part of the product story;
- AI is framed as creative acceleration while the user still decides what to keep.

Useful for Fluffy:
The gallery should feel like a studio table full of directions, not a results dashboard.

### 4. Claude Design
https://claude.com/product/design

What it teaches:
- AI design can be sold around iteration and staying on brand;
- human control is part of the promise;
- the handoff between agent and visual canvas is treated as continuous.

Useful for Fluffy:
Make the owner-review loop feel intentional and premium rather than a fallback after automation.

### 5. Spline
https://spline.design/

What it teaches:
- 3D earns attention when the object itself is the product experience;
- the site uses spatial examples early;
- interactive depth can still live inside a clear product page.

Useful for Fluffy:
Three.js candidates should use a metaphor that changes as the marketing story sharpens. Do not make a star field and call it innovation.

## Product / developer brands

### 6. Linear
https://linear.app/

What it teaches:
- very controlled information density;
- product screenshots and interface behavior carry much of the persuasion;
- restraint creates confidence;
- copy is usually short enough that visuals can breathe.

Useful for Fluffy:
One candidate should be extremely restrained and product-led to test whether Fluffy needs less copy after the research is done.

### 7. Stripe
https://stripe.com/

What it teaches:
- technical depth can coexist with polished commercial storytelling;
- complex products are explained through concrete flows and examples;
- visual systems can be expressive while remaining tied to product mechanics.

Useful for Fluffy:
Market sophistication does not require turning the page into a methodology document.

### 8. Vercel
https://vercel.com/

What it teaches:
- strong product hierarchy and terse copy;
- developer audience is trusted to understand technical terms when they are useful;
- live product and performance signals appear as proof.

Useful for Fluffy:
Write to the actual audience instead of flattening everything into generic plain-English SaaS language.

### 9. Cursor
https://cursor.com/

What it teaches:
- developer tools can feel aspirational without abandoning code and product artifacts;
- brand is strongly tied to the act of building.

Useful for Fluffy:
A technical founder page can show repo, diff, prompt, or generated artifact as the hero visual.

### 10. Raycast
https://www.raycast.com/

What it teaches:
- a utility product can have personality through illustration, motion, and tone;
- product screenshots remain central even with a strong brand layer.

Useful for Fluffy:
Personality should decorate product understanding, not replace it.

## AI / media / creative brands

### 11. ElevenLabs
https://elevenlabs.io/

What it teaches:
- media output itself becomes proof;
- AI product value is experienced rather than described at length.

Useful for Fluffy:
The best demo is often the result. Let visitors compare the generic draft, the researched brief, and the final page.

### 12. Runway
https://runwayml.com/

What it teaches:
- creative AI can use cinematic presentation because the output category supports it;
- visual work receives more space than explanatory copy.

Useful for Fluffy:
A cinematic candidate is valid when the product has something visually worth dramatizing.

### 13. Anthropic
https://www.anthropic.com/

What it teaches:
- editorial typography and a quieter palette can signal seriousness in AI;
- heavy visual effects are not necessary for a premium AI brand.

Useful for Fluffy:
One direction should feel like an editorial publication rather than a SaaS landing page.

### 14. Granola
https://www.granola.ai/

What it teaches:
- warm, human product presentation can make AI feel like a practical collaborator;
- the brand does not need generic sci-fi imagery to signal intelligence.

Useful for Fluffy:
Humanity can come from tone, material, photography/illustration choices, and product scenario rather than fake conversational slang.

## Commercial storytelling / GTM brands

### 15. Clay
https://www.clay.com/

What it teaches:
- current homepage work is explicitly built around a market category story;
- real customer outcomes make ambitious claims believable;
- Clay built an interactive homepage experience to create an early `aha` moment;
- use cases are tied to revenue consequences instead of feature inventory.

Reference:
https://www.clay.com/blog/new-homepage-2026

Useful for Fluffy:
Every major page should have one moment where the visitor can see the product idea working instead of reading another paragraph about it.

### 16. Ramp
https://ramp.com/

What it teaches:
- business outcomes can be made concrete through numbers and real workflows;
- dense product scope can still be organized around buyer consequences.

Useful for Fluffy:
Numbers should be large enough to matter and close enough to the claim to feel like proof.

### 17. Attio
https://attio.com/

What it teaches:
- interface-led storytelling can feel editorial and modern;
- structure can be precise without looking like a dashboard template.

Useful for Fluffy:
The page itself can feel like a crafted object rather than a stack of interchangeable marketing sections.

### 18. Pitch
https://pitch.com/

What it teaches:
- presentation is treated as a visual product, so examples and artifacts dominate;
- gallery/browse behavior is a natural way to sell creative possibility.

Useful for Fluffy:
The review gallery is part of the product story. Make choosing between directions feel enjoyable.

### 19. Gamma
https://gamma.app/

What it teaches:
- AI-created artifacts are sold through speed and breadth of output;
- many use cases can be communicated through visual examples.

Useful for Fluffy:
Fluffy's differentiation needs to live upstream in research and market judgment because artifact generation itself is already crowded.

## Editorial / outside software

### 20. Readymag
https://readymag.com/

What it teaches:
- websites can feel closer to editorial design than application UI;
- typography and composition can carry a whole identity.

Useful for Fluffy:
Break the SaaS-section habit. Some candidate pages should feel authored page by page.

### 21. Bloomberg Graphics
https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/

What it teaches:
- data and narrative can share one visual flow;
- numbers become memorable when they are tied to a visual argument.

Useful for Fluffy:
Market research can be visual storytelling instead of a row of statistic cards.

### 22. The New Yorker
https://www.newyorker.com/

What it teaches:
- a strong editorial voice can hold attention with relatively simple page mechanics;
- illustration can create a distinctive point of view.

Useful for Fluffy:
Human-sounding copy needs an editorial point of view, not merely lower reading level.

### 23. NASA
https://www.nasa.gov/

What it teaches:
- mission framing gives complex technical systems a clear human goal;
- diagrams and imagery make engineering stakes understandable.

Useful for Fluffy:
PAM can borrow preflight/mission language carefully because the product really concerns readiness before execution.

### 24. Scientific instrument / lab interfaces
No single mandatory site. Look at spectroscopy, calibration, observatory, and measurement-system references for Research Assurance.

What it teaches:
- multiple independent readings can be made legible without pretending they collapse into one score;
- physical instruments give `assurance` a more active and scientific visual world than compliance dashboards.

Useful for Fluffy:
Research Assurance should feel like a calibration lab or measurement stack, not an audit portal.

## Chosen v2 Fluffy directions

### A. Editorial Cut

Emotional target:
Sharp, human, literate, skeptical of fluff.

Visual world:
Magazine spread, red pencil, copy edits, paper texture, oversized black type, sparse red annotation.

Product demo:
Bad first draft appears beside the researched rewrite and buyer brief.

References:
Anthropic, Readymag, New Yorker, Bloomberg Graphics.

### B. Creative Studio

Emotional target:
Exploratory, tactile, optimistic, design-literate.

Visual world:
Pinned brand references, swatches, browser windows, notes, copy scraps, moveable-card energy.

Product demo:
Product repo enters the board. Audience language, competitors, stats, and brand territories appear around it before candidate pages emerge.

References:
Figma, Replit Design, Framer, Pitch.

### C. Product Showroom

Emotional target:
Confident, commercial, fast, premium.

Visual world:
Dark or neutral stage, large live product workflow, minimal copy, crisp interface, one bright accent.

Product demo:
Repo -> research brief -> four page candidates in one interactive strip.

References:
Linear, Vercel, Stripe, Clay.

### D. Fuzzy to Sharp 3D

Emotional target:
Memorable, playful, intelligent.

Visual world:
Soft irregular 3D forms representing raw product information move into a clean spatial composition as buyer, market, proof, and brand context arrive.

Product demo:
The scene resolves into a finished page structure. Hover/touch exposes the research source behind each object.

References:
Spline, Runway, Figma's more expressive 2026 canvas work.

## Anti-reference

Avoid the default 2024-2026 AI startup page:
- black background;
- purple/blue glowing orb;
- glass cards;
- random particle field;
- tiny monospace labels;
- five feature cards;
- repeated `not X, but Y` slogans;
- abstract `intelligence layer` language;
- no actual buyer or product shown.

That combination now communicates category sameness more than innovation.
