# Brand research protocol

A Fluffy run should research visual territory before designing a major page.

The goal is to understand how the market currently looks, what emotional codes already belong to the category, and where a product can feel distinctive without looking random.

## Minimum reference set

For a substantial redesign, inspect at least 20 live references.

Split them across:

### Direct category
Products a buyer might compare directly.

### Adjacent emotional category
Products that create the same feeling even if they solve a different job.

### Editorial / storytelling
Sites that handle narrative, case-study pacing, typography, or evidence well.

### Interaction / motion
Sites with useful product demos, scroll behavior, canvas, WebGL, or Three.js ideas.

### Outside software
Editorial design, industrial design, magazines, architecture, packaging, museums, fashion, scientific instruments, games, or other relevant visual cultures.

## What to record

For each reference, capture:

```text
REFERENCE:
AUDIENCE:
FIRST-SCREEN PROMISE:
EMOTIONAL TONE:
TYPOGRAPHY:
COMPOSITION:
COLOR ROLE:
PRODUCT VISUALIZATION:
MOTION / INTERACTION:
WHAT TO LEARN:
WHAT TO AVOID COPYING:
```

Do not save a list of links with no analysis.

## Distinctive direction rule

Develop at least three visual territories before rendering candidates.

A territory must answer:

- What should the visitor feel?
- What physical or cultural world does this borrow from?
- What is the signature visual device?
- How is the product shown?
- What does motion mean?
- What does the design refuse to do?

Example territories for Fluffy:

### Editorial cutting room
Feels opinionated, literate, sharp, human. Oversized editorial typography, red-pencil marks, source clippings, before/after copy, rough margin notes, restrained motion.

### Creative studio wall
Feels exploratory and tactile. Moodboard composition, swatches, pinned references, browser windows, copy fragments, brand boards, drag-like motion, layered paper and screen surfaces.

### Fuzzy to sharp 3D
Feels playful and memorable. Soft 3D forms enter messy and resolve into crisp type, grids, or product cards as the story becomes clearer. Use actual transformation as the visual metaphor. Avoid generic particle clouds.

### Product showroom
Feels premium and commercial. A live repo-to-market workflow is the hero. Sparse typography, product demo front and center, high-contrast interface, minimal decoration.

These are four different worlds. A candidate set should feel this different.

## Brand-system output

After research, save a compact direction brief for each candidate:

```text
NAME:
EMOTION:
AUDIENCE FIT:
VISUAL METAPHOR:
TYPE DIRECTION:
COLOR LOGIC:
PRODUCT VISUAL:
MOTION:
3D ROLE:
REFERENCE INFLUENCES:
ANTI-REFERENCES:
```

Color hex values come after this decision.

## Design-quality questions

Before publish:

- Does the first screen look specific to this product?
- Is there a real visual idea beyond cards and gradients?
- Does the product appear early enough?
- Does motion clarify something?
- Is there meaningful contrast between candidate directions?
- Would the design still make sense if the current copy disappeared?
- Would the copy still make sense if the visual effects failed?

A strong page should survive both tests.
