# Market and audience research

Fluffy should not infer the market from the product architecture alone.

## Research target

For each product, answer:

- Who feels the problem first?
- What are they trying to get done?
- What event makes the problem urgent?
- What are they doing today?
- What do they dislike about the current approach?
- What result are they willing to switch or pay for?
- Which adjacent roles care for different reasons?

## Competitive alternatives

Start with what the buyer would do if this product disappeared tomorrow.

Include:
- direct competitors
- existing internal tools
- spreadsheets / docs / manual processes
- general AI tools
- consultants or agencies
- doing nothing

Do not define competition only by product category.

April Dunford's positioning work is a useful reference here:
- https://www.aprildunford.com/post/a-quickstart-guide-to-positioning
- https://www.aprildunford.com/post/positioning-and-competition

## Audience language

Search at least two live sources where the audience speaks naturally.

Useful sources:
- Reddit
- Hacker News
- GitHub issues and discussions
- G2 / Capterra / app-store reviews
- Stack Overflow or domain forums
- public Slack/Discord archives when searchable
- conference talks and practitioner blogs
- customer support threads

Collect:
- repeated frustrations
- repeated workarounds
- phrases used to describe success
- objections
- buying triggers
- words the audience uses for the category

Separate direct quotes from paraphrases.

## Quantitative market evidence

Use at least two credible sources when the product story benefits from market scale or trend data.

Good sources:
- peer-reviewed studies
- public datasets
- official company research with methodology
- established market-research firms
- surveys with sample size and date

Avoid unattributed statistics from SEO pages.

Record:

```text
STAT:
SOURCE:
DATE:
SAMPLE / METHOD:
WHAT IT SUPPORTS:
WHAT IT DOES NOT SUPPORT:
```

## Why now

A useful `why now` is a real change in the environment.

Examples:
- foundation models made a previously expensive capability cheap
- a workflow moved from human-only to AI-assisted
- a regulation changed buying requirements
- a category matured enough for a new layer
- a data source became accessible
- a cost curve changed

Avoid generic lines about the world moving faster.

## Market-gap test

The final market-gap sentence should survive these questions:

1. Does the target buyer recognize the current workflow?
2. Are the alternatives described fairly?
3. Is the gap specific enough that another random startup cannot claim it?
4. Does the product actually address the gap today?
5. Is the gap large enough to matter beyond one internal implementation detail?

## Example: Fluffy itself

Current alternatives include coding agents, Framer, Replit Design, Figma Sites, Claude Design, agencies, freelance copywriters, and founders writing their own pages.

Those tools and services cover different parts of creation extremely well. Current public positioning from Framer, Replit, and Figma emphasizes generating, refining, designing, and publishing web experiences. The Fluffy workflow concentrates on the upstream product-marketing research that decides which buyer pain, competitive alternative, evidence, and brand territory should drive the generated page.

Useful current references:
- https://www.framer.com/design/
- https://replit.com/design
- https://www.figma.com/sites/
- https://claude.com/blog/claude-design-stays-on-brand-for-daily-work

Market context:
- Ahrefs surveyed 879 marketers in 2025 and reported 87% use AI to help create content; users of AI published 42% more content per month: https://ahrefs.com/blog/marketers-using-ai-publish-more-content/
- Gartner surveyed 418 marketing leaders in 2024; among organizations using GenAI, 77% used it for creative development: https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-02-18-gartner-survey-reveals-over-a-quarter-of-marketing-organizations-have-limited-or-no-adoption-of-genai-for-marketing-campaigns
- A Journal of Business Research paper reported seven preregistered experiments where perceived AI authorship of emotional marketing communications reduced positive word of mouth and loyalty: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbusres.2024.114984

These sources support a market where AI-assisted creative production is common and authenticity/quality still matters. They do not prove Fluffy improves conversion or authenticity.
