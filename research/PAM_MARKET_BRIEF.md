# PAM market brief

## Product truth

Project Assurance Modules (PAM) is a versioned methodology registry for moving sufficiently researched ideas into engineered projects without depending on a human or agent to remember every planning, validation, governance, continuity, reuse, and evidence obligation.

It is not a runtime agent-control service and not a universal quality stamp.

Its most useful human question is simpler than its architecture:

> Before research becomes a repo, issues, architecture, code, benchmarks, or a release, what important engineering work are we forgetting?

## Real product incident

A real PAM adoption in `Pukujan/interview-os-game` exposed a loophole in the first build-vs-reuse methodology.

An agent could create a plausible-looking candidate register and satisfy the wording without proving that it actually searched a serious candidate universe. The most bespoke capability was compared against an abstract `generic quiz framework`, PAM itself appeared as a candidate even though it is methodology rather than a product alternative, and reusable dataset discovery was not required.

That incident produced PAM issue #10 and the hardened `projectization.build-vs-reuse@0.2.0` contract.

The marketing lesson is not "PAM has eight reuse requirements."

It is:

> A checklist can look complete while the important work never happened.

## Audience

### Primary

Technical founders, senior engineers, research engineers, and AI-assisted builders taking a nontrivial idea from research/exploration into implementation.

### Secondary

- engineering leads reviewing AI-generated project plans;
- agent/platform teams that need repeatable project handoffs;
- research-heavy teams that cannot afford benchmark, scope, reuse, or validation decisions to live only in chat context.

## Audience language

Useful recognizable moments:

- "Are we actually ready to build this?"
- "Did we research alternatives or just write down a decision?"
- "Why are we opening issues before we agree what success means?"
- "Who decided this benchmark was good enough?"
- "The next agent is going to ask all of this again."
- "We shipped a lot of code. Did we solve the right problem?"

## Market timing

AI has made implementation dramatically easier to start, while confidence in higher-responsibility planning and verification remains much weaker.

The 2025 Stack Overflow Developer Survey reports:

- **84%** of respondents use or plan to use AI tools in development;
- **46%** distrust AI-tool accuracy versus **33%** who trust it;
- **66%** cite "almost right" AI solutions as a frustration;
- **69%** do not plan to use AI for project planning;
- **87%** of respondents express concern about AI-agent accuracy.

Source: https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/ai

DORA's 2025/2026 reporting describes AI as an amplifier of the underlying software-delivery system. Its 2026 analysis says AI can increase throughput while increasing instability when the surrounding foundation is weak, and emphasizes strong user focus, testing, delivery fundamentals, and governance rather than code generation alone.

Sources:

- https://dora.dev/research/publications/
- https://dora.dev/insights/balancing-ai-tensions/
- https://dora.dev/capabilities/user-centric-focus/

## Competitive / substitute frame

PAM does not need to claim that project-management or AI-planning tools do not exist.

The practical alternatives are:

- founder/senior-engineer memory;
- chat instructions and long system prompts;
- project templates;
- issue templates;
- architecture checklists;
- ADR practices;
- generic agent planning modes;
- platform-specific workflow rules;
- internal engineering playbooks.

PAM's potential wedge is the transition boundary:

> reusable, versioned methodology that routes only the assurance work that applies, freezes what the project intends to prove, and asks for evidence-backed closure rather than a persuasive planning document.

## Positioning candidates

### A. Project preflight

**Hero idea:** You finished the research. Are you actually ready to build?

Strength: instantly understandable, good visual metaphor, keeps PAM out of governance jargon.

Risk: "preflight" is used broadly in software and can sound like another checklist unless the evidence-circularity story proves the difference.

### B. The project starts before the repo

**Hero idea:** The first commit is already too late for some decisions.

Strength: founder/engineering-lead tone, emphasizes research-to-project transition.

Risk: can sound dogmatic if phrased too absolutely.

### C. Make the plan prove its homework

**Hero idea:** A convincing plan is not proof the research happened.

Strength: directly grounded in the Interview OS defect and extremely relevant to agent-generated planning.

Risk: narrower wedge; needs expansion from reuse research into the broader PAM methodology.

## Recommended candidate mix

Do not pick one story yet. The gallery should test all three:

1. **Launch Preflight** — hopeful, decisive, warm industrial visual language.
2. **Blueprint Before Build** — editorial/architectural paper system.
3. **Mission Control 3D** — spatial gates showing a project moving from idea to launch only after clearing scope, reuse, evidence, and handoff.
4. **Proof of Homework** — bold human/agent story built around the real issue #10 incident and AI-era market numbers.

## Claims to avoid

- PAM guarantees project success.
- PAM proves a research conclusion is true.
- PAM eliminates human review.
- PAM is a universal agent governance protocol.
- Teams using PAM ship faster or with fewer defects unless measured later.
- The Interview OS incident proves population-level outcomes.
